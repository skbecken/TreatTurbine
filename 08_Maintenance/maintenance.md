# 08 - Maintenance

## What you can find here

- A post-flight inspection checklist covering the drone and TreatTurbine
- Battery care, handling, and storage tips
- Identification and management of TreatTurbine mechanical wear items

Note: These guidelines reflect the operational experience of Team 223. Users should adapt inspection frequency and maintenance practices based on mission profile, environment, and hardware configuration.

---

## Post-Flight Inspection

### Drone Airframe & Systems

- Inspect frame, arms, and landing gear for cracks, deformation, or loose joints
- Check propellers for chips, bends, or surface damage
- Verify all visible fasteners are secure and have not backed out due to vibration
- Inspect wiring and connectors for fraying, pinching, or partial disconnection
- Check payload mounting interface for rigidity and signs of shifting during flight

### Electronics

- Check battery for swelling, physical damage, or abnormal heat
- Inspect power leads and connectors for discoloration or looseness
- Confirm GPS, receiver, and telemetry modules remain securely mounted
- Observe any unusual odors or heat concentration near electronics

### TreatTurbine Dispenser

- Inspect all printed components for cracks, layer separation, or warping
- Manually rotate the carousel if possible to confirm smooth, consistent motion
- Check for wobble or misalignment in the drum assembly
- Inspect pawl engagement for consistent, positive indexing
- Verify no debris, dust, or treat residue is obstructing motion

### Functionality Check

- Perform a ground-based actuation test
- Confirm:
  - Smooth indexing of the carousel
  - Single, consistent dispense per actuation
  - No skipping, binding, or double-dispense behavior

### Post-Flight Notes

- Record any irregularities observed during flight (missed drops, delayed actuation, increased vibration, etc.)
- Flag components that may require closer inspection or replacement

## Battery Care and Storage

### General Handling

- Do not recharge batteries that are swollen, punctured, or otherwise damaged
- Avoid excessive mechanical stress on battery leads and connectors

### Charging

- Use a balance charger appropriate for LiPo batteries
- Do not leave batteries unattended while charging
- Ensure proper cell count and voltage settings before initiating charge

### Post-Flight Management

- Do not store batteries fully charged or fully depleted
- Bring batteries to a safe storage voltage after use
- Monitor for abnormal heat or voltage drop after flight

### Storage

- Store batteries in a cool, dry environment away from direct sunlight
- Use fire-resistant storage bags when possible
- Keep batteries away from flammable materials

## Mechanical Wear Items (TreatTurbine)

### Pawl

- Primary wear component due to repeated engagement with gear ring
- Inspect for rounding, deformation, or reduced engagement depth
- Replace if slipping, skipping, or inconsistent indexing is observed

### Carousel / Drum

- Check for warping or uneven rotation resistance
- Inspect contact surfaces for wear or buildup that increases friction
- Reprint if binding occurs or tolerances degrade, material change could be needed

### Gear Ring

- Inspect teeth for wear, chipping, or deformation
- Ensure consistent engagement with pawl across full rotation
- Replace if uneven motion or skipping occurs

### Fasteners in Printed Parts

- Monitor for loosening or thread degradation in printed holes
- Replace stripped components or consider thread inserts if repeated wear occurs

---

## General Maintenance Notes

- Clean components regularly to prevent buildup of dust, debris, or treat residue
- Avoid exposing printed parts to prolonged high temperatures or direct sunlight
- Reprint components showing structural fatigue or dimensional instability
- Maintain a small inventory of critical spare parts (pawl, drum, fasteners)
